Today's session

Monday introduced the seven day-of-the-week kanji. Today you get the grammar to use them in real sentences — time particles. Japanese uses three key time patterns you'll encounter on every N5 paper: に (at/on), から (from), and まで (until). Plus the frequency words 毎日・毎週・毎月・毎年, which appear in natural speech constantly.

The trickiest thing about に is knowing when not to use it — relative time words like 今日, 毎日, 明日 never take に. That's the gap that trips people up, and it's exactly what you'll drill today.

Everything here is N5 core. By the end you'll be able to express any daily schedule, timetable, or routine — the building blocks for real conversation about time.

PART 01

The time particle に

~8 min

に marks a specific, fixed point in time: a clock time, a day of the week, a month, a year. Think of it as pinning an action to an exact spot on a calendar or clock.

[Specific time] に [action]
at / on / in — pinned to a fixed time

に works with anything you can point to on a clock or calendar:

  • 七時に起きます。 しちじに おきます I wake up at seven o'clock.
  • 月曜日にクラスがあります。 げつようびに クラスが あります I have class on Monday.
  • 三月に日本語を始めました。 さんがつに にほんごを はじめました I started Japanese in March.
  • 二〇二五年に日本へ行きたいです。 にせんにじゅうごねんに にほんへ いきたいです I want to go to Japan in 2025.
The に trap — relative time words never take に
Words like 今日, 明日, 昨日, 今週, 来週, 先週, 毎日, 今 are relative — they shift depending on when you say them, so they can't be "pinned." Drop に completely with these words.

✗ 今日に学校へ行きます — wrong
✓ 今日学校へ行きます — correct

✗ 毎日にコーヒーを飲みます — wrong
✓ 毎日コーヒーを飲みます — correct

Expressionに?Notes
七時✓ にspecific clock time → に
月曜日✓ にspecific day of week → に

PART 02

から and まで

~7 min

から marks the starting point (from, since, after). まで marks the end point (until, up to, as far as). They work for time, place, and sequence — and you can use one or both.

[A] から [B] まで [action]
from A to B · starting point → ending point
  • 九時から五時まで働きます。 くじから ごじまで はたらきます I work from 9 to 5.
  • 月曜日から金曜日まで授業があります。 げつようびから きんようびまで じゅぎょうが あります There are classes from Monday to Friday.
  • 今から始めます。 いまから はじめます I'll start from now. / Starting now.
  • 六月まで勉強します。 ろくがつまで べんきょうします I'll study until June.
  • 子供のときから日本語が好きです。 こどものときから にほんごが すきです I've liked Japanese since childhood.
から alone vs まで alone
You don't always need both. から alone = "starting from" (open-ended); まで alone = "up until" (open start). 今から = "from now on" (no specified end). 六月まで = "until June" (started at some unspecified earlier point). Both are natural in everyday speech.
PART 03

毎～ — frequency expressions

~5 min

毎 (まい) means "every" and attaches directly to a time word. These are among the most commonly used time expressions in Japanese — you'll use them every day in basic conversation.

毎～ frequency words
KanjiReadingMeaningNotes
毎日まいにちevery daymost common of the set
毎週まいしゅうevery week週 = week (kanji)
毎月まいつきevery month月 here reads つき (kun), not ガツ
毎年まいとし / まいねんevery yearboth readings used; まいとし slightly more conversational
毎朝まいあさevery morning朝 = morning
毎晩まいばんevery evening晩 = evening (not on N5, but common)
  • 毎日日本語を練習しています。 まいにち にほんごを れんしゅうしています I practice Japanese every day.
  • 毎週月曜日にクラスがあります。 まいしゅう げつようびに クラスが あります I have class every Monday.
  • 毎年桜を見に行きます。 まいとし さくらを みに いきます I go to see the cherry blossoms every year.
毎月 readings — a small trap
毎月 reads まいつき in everyday speech, not まいがつ. This is because 月 takes its kun reading (つき) rather than the on reading (ガツ) when paired with 毎. Same kanji, different context = different reading. Compare: 来月 (らいげつ, next month) vs 毎月 (まいつき, every month). Both are common; both use 月 differently.
PART 04

Time-of-day vocabulary

~4 min

These words let you anchor events to a part of the day. Wednesday's session goes deep on the time kanji (時・分・半); here's just enough to make the drills work today.

Time-of-day words
KanjiReadingMeaning
朝あさmorning (roughly dawn–10am)
昼ひるnoon / daytime (10am–3pm or so)
夕方ゆうがたlate afternoon, early evening
夜よるevening / night (after dark)
午前ごぜんAM (before noon) — formal/official
午後ごごPM (after noon) — formal/official
〜時〜じo'clock: 一時 (いちじ) = 1 o'clock
〜分〜ふん / 〜ぷんminutes (sound changes — see Wednesday)
半はんhalf past: 三時半 = half past 3

Note that 午前・午後 come before the clock time in Japanese — the opposite of Danish/English: 午後三時 (ごごさんじ) = 3 PM.

DRILL

Active practice

~8 min

Do these aloud. The に trap especially needs to be practiced out loud so the correct instinct becomes automatic — reading silently doesn't build that.

Drill 1 · に or no に?

For each expression, decide: does it take に or not? Say your answer aloud before checking.

  1. 月曜日＿クラスがあります。
  2. 今日＿何をしますか。
  3. 三時＿帰ります。
  4. 毎週＿泳ぎます。
  5. 来週＿友達に会います。
  6. 十二月＿コペンハーゲンに来ました。
  7. 昨日＿映画を見ました。
  8. 七時半＿起きます。
月曜日に ✓ · 今日は (no に) ✗ · 三時に ✓ · 毎週 (no に) ✗ · 来週 (no に) ✗ · 十二月に ✓ · 昨日 (no に) ✗ · 七時半に ✓

Drill 2 · から and まで

Build each sentence aloud using から, まで, or から～まで.

  1. "I study from 8 to 10." (八時, 十時, 勉強します)
  2. "I work from Monday to Friday." (月曜日, 金曜日, 働きます)
  3. "I'll do it until tomorrow." (明日まで, します)
  4. "Starting from now, I'll try hard." (今から, 頑張ります)
  5. "Class is from 6 PM to 8 PM." (午後六時, 午後八時, 授業があります)
八時から十時まで勉強します · 月曜日から金曜日まで働きます · 明日まで(に)します · 今から頑張ります · 午後六時から午後八時まで授業があります

Drill 3 · 毎～ sentences

Translate each sentence aloud using the right 毎～ word.

  1. "I drink coffee every morning." (コーヒーを飲みます)
  2. "I have Japanese class every week." (日本語のクラスがあります)
  3. "I go to Denmark every year." (デンマークへ行きます)
  4. "I eat lunch every day." (昼ごはんを食べます)
毎朝コーヒーを飲みます · 毎週日本語のクラスがあります · 毎年デンマークへ行きます · 毎日昼ごはんを食べます

Drill 4 · Read these schedules aloud

These are complete sentences describing a weekly routine. Read each one aloud at full pace — combining today's particles with Monday's day-of-week kanji.

  1. 月曜日から水曜日まで、毎日日本語を勉強します。
  2. 木曜日の午後七時にクラスがあります。
  3. 毎週土曜日に図書館へ行きます。
  4. 今日は何曜日ですか。 — then answer with today's day
  5. 日曜日は朝から晩まで休みます。
げつようびから すいようびまで、まいにち にほんごを べんきょうします · もくようびの ごごしちじに クラスが あります · まいしゅう どようびに としょかんへ いきます · きょうは なんようびですか · にちようびは あさから ばんまで やすみます

Particles locked in. You can schedule anything now.

に, から, まで, and 毎～ are the four pieces that turn a raw time word into a sentence. With these and Monday's seven day kanji, you can describe a full weekly schedule in natural Japanese — which is exactly the kind of sentence that appears in the N5 reading section.

The main thing to practice this week: notice the に trap in the wild. When you write or speak a Japanese sentence with a time word, ask yourself: is this a fixed point (→ use に) or a relative/frequency word (→ drop に)? That instinct is what the test actually checks.
