Today's session

Wednesday is the longest session of the week. You start with a quick handwriting pass over Monday's seven day kanji — three minutes, one write of each — then meet the six kanji that complete the calendar system: 曜 年 時 分 半 今.

Together, all thirteen give you full control over Japanese dates, clock times, and schedules. The session pays most attention to 時 and 分 — the building blocks of telling time — and to 分's sound changes, which follow exactly the same rule as ～本 from Week 1 Tuesday: 1, 6, 8, 10 → っぷ; 3 → んぷ.

By the end you should be able to read any time on a clock in Japanese and construct sentences like 今日は午後三時半にクラスがあります without hesitation.

KANJI 21

曜 — day of the week

~4 min
曜
Number 21 of 104
day of the week
音ヨウ
訓(なし)

Structure & mnemonic

The most complex kanji this week at 18 strokes. The left side holds 日 (sun), which marks it as time-related. The right side contains 隹 (a bird radical) and 羽 (wings/feathers) — the sun shining through the wings of a bird cycling through its weekly route. The cycling bird = the cycling days of the week.

At N5 level you need to recognise 曜 instantly, not necessarily reproduce it from memory. It only ever appears in the pattern ～曜日 (day of the week).

Stroke count

18 strokes — by far the most this week. Write it slowly once; don't rush it.

Example words

  • 曜日ようび— day of the week (in general)
  • 何曜日なんようび— what day of the week?
  • 月曜日げつようび— Monday (moon-day)
  • 火曜日かようび— Tuesday (fire-day)
Recognition priority: When you see 曜 on a timetable, poster, or schedule, your brain should fire "day of the week" immediately. That reflex is the goal — faster than reading the furigana.
KANJI 22

年 — year

~3 min
年
Number 22 of 104
year
音ネン
訓とし

Mnemonic

The original pictograph showed a person (人) carrying a heavy bundle of grain stalks at harvest time. One harvest = one year. Today the character is simplified, but the bent person struggling under a load is still visible in the upper strokes — the weight of a year passing.

Stroke count

6 strokes

Example words

  • 今年ことし— this year (IRREGULAR — not こんねん)
  • 来年らいねん— next year
  • 去年きょねん— last year (IRREGULAR — not さくねん)
  • 毎年まいとし— every year (also まいねん)
  • 何年なんねん— what year?
  • 二〇二五年ねん— the year 2025
Two irregular readings: 今年 = ことし (not こんねん) and 去年 = きょねん (not さくねん). Both are daily words that every Japanese speaker says automatically in their spoken, not on'yomi, forms.
KANJI 23

時 — time / hour / o'clock

~4 min
時
Number 23 of 104
time · hour · o'clock
音ジ
訓とき

Mnemonic

日 (sun) on the left + 寺 (temple) on the right. In ancient Japan, the temple bell rang every hour — the monks tracked time by the sun's position and announced it with bells. The character is the bell tower watching the sun. A clock made of sunlight and bronze.

Stroke count

10 strokes

Example words

  • 何時なんじ— what time? (o'clock)
  • 三時じ— 3 o'clock (specific clock point)
  • 時間じかん— duration / a period of time
  • 三時間じかん— for 3 hours (duration, not point)
  • 時々ときどき— sometimes (reduplicated)
  • 〜の時とき— when ~, at the time of ~
時 vs 時間: 三時 (さんじ) = "at 3 o'clock" — a specific point. 三時間 (さんじかん) = "for 3 hours" — a duration. These are different words and they take different particles: 三時に (at 3 o'clock) vs 三時間 働きました (I worked for 3 hours — no に).
KANJI 24

分 — minute / part / understand

~6 min
分
Number 24 of 104
minute · part · understand
音フン・ブン・フ
訓わか(る)・わ(ける)

Mnemonic

刀 (knife/blade) cutting through 八 (the "split" shape) — something being divided into parts. A minute divides an hour; a fraction divides a whole; understanding divides confusion from clarity. Same kanji, three layers of meaning.

Stroke count

4 strokes

Sound changes for ～分 (minutes)

This is where 分 gets interesting. The same rule as ～本: after 1, 6, 8, 10 the sound doubles to っぷ; after 3, it voices to んぷ. Drill these until automatic.

2二分にふんregular
3三分さんぷん→ んぷ
4四分よんふんregular
5五分ごふんregular
6六分ろっぷん→ っぷ
7七分ななふんregular
8八分はっぷん→ っぷ

Example words

  • 三時十五分ふん— 3:15 (three fifteen)
  • 半分はんぶん— half (a portion) — ぶん reading here
  • 十分じゅうぶん— enough / sufficient (different reading: じゅうぶん, not じゅっぷん)
  • 分わかります— I understand (kun reading)
十分 ambiguity: 十分 can read じゅっぷん (ten minutes) or じゅうぶん (enough, sufficient) — context resolves it every time. "This is 十分" in a time context = 10 minutes. "This is 十分" in a sufficiency context = enough. No one ever actually confuses them in practice.
KANJI 25

半 — half

~3 min
半
Number 25 of 104
half
音ハン
訓なかば

Mnemonic

The character 十 (ten) being split apart by the two diagonal strokes of 八 (eight, also meaning "split" in etymology) — a number cut cleanly in two. A cross being pulled apart at the arms. 5 strokes, very clean shape.

Stroke count

5 strokes

Example words

  • 三時半はん— 3:30 (half past three) — always means exactly :30
  • 半分はんぶん— half (of something) — a portion, not a time
  • 一時間半はん— an hour and a half (1.5 hours)
  • 半年はんとし— half a year, six months
〜時半 only means :30: If you want to say 3:45, you can't say 三時と四分の三 — you just say 三時四十五分 (さんじよんじゅうごふん). 半 is only used for exact half-hours. Don't overextend it.
KANJI 26

今 — now / present

~3 min
今
Number 26 of 104
now · present
音コン・キン
訓いま

Mnemonic

A roof (亠) sheltering something gathered underneath — whatever is happening right now is held here, in this moment, under this roof. The "gathering into one place" form suggests everything converging on the present instant. 4 strokes, one of the most written characters in daily Japanese.

Stroke count

4 strokes

Example words

  • 今いま— now, right now
  • 今日きょう— today (IRREGULAR — not こんにち)
  • 今週こんしゅう— this week
  • 今月こんげつ— this month
  • 今年ことし— this year (IRREGULAR — not こんねん)
  • 今からいまから— from now on / starting now
Irregular compound readings: 今日 = きょう (not こんにち — that's only in こんにちは), 今年 = ことし (not こんねん). Both are extremely common in speech. The formal/written forms こんにち and こんねん exist but are rarely used in everyday conversation. Learn きょう and ことし first.
REFERENCE

Telling time in Japanese

Learn this pattern

Japanese clock time follows the pattern: [午前/午後] + [〜時] + [〜分]. AM/PM comes first (opposite of English). Minutes come after the hour, never before.

WrittenReadingEnglish
今何時ですか。 いまなんじですか What time is it now?
三時です。 さんじです It is 3 o'clock.
三時半です。 さんじはんです It is 3:30.
三時十五分です。 さんじじゅうごふんです It is 3:15.
午後三時四十五分です。 ごごさんじよんじゅうごふんです It is 3:45 PM.
午前九時から始まります。 ごぜんくじからはじまります It starts from 9 AM.
十二時に昼ごはんを食べます。 じゅうにじにひるごはんをたべます I eat lunch at 12 o'clock.

Thirteen kanji. The full calendar in your hands.

With Monday's seven element kanji and today's six time kanji, you now have the complete Japanese date-and-time system. Any clock face, any timetable, any school schedule, any meeting invite — you can read all of it. That's the payoff from three sessions of focused work.

The 分 sound changes are your main takeaway — drill those specifically over the next few days. They appear in every clock-time conversation and in the JLPT listening section with no warning. The distinction between 時 (point in time, takes に) and 時間 (duration, no に) is the second priority.
