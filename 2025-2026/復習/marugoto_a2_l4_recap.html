Lesson 4 · どうやって食べますか

How do you eat this? · Food etiquette & instructions
Topic 2
4 grammar patterns
Episode 4

About this lesson

Lesson 4 focuses on how to eat Japanese food — the methods, manners, and rules. Rupa asks how to eat tofu steak; Abe explains step by step. The lesson naturally introduces four patterns for sequence, method, prohibition, and natural consequences.

You learn V-てから for ordered actions ("after doing A, do B"), V-て / V-ないで for describing how an action is done ("eat it with the sauce / without adding salt"), and V-ると for natural results. You also pick up the prohibition pattern V-ちゃだめ and its written form V-てはいけません.

Cultural highlight: the formal dining expressions いただきます (before eating) and ごちそうさまでした (after eating) are explained in context.

Grammar

Four Patterns

てから · ないで · ちゃだめ · と
V-ては いけません / V-ちゃ だめ
must not ~ / you can't ~
V-て + は いけません (written/formal) · V-ちゃ だめです (spoken/casual) · negative verb: V-なくちゃ いけません (must do)
Prohibition: something is not allowed or should not be done. Casual ちゃ/じゃ is a contraction of ては/では. Use てはいけません in formal writing or when speaking to someone unfamiliar.
  • なべのふたはまだとっちゃだめです。 You can't take the lid off the pot yet. (casual)
  • 車くるまを運転うんてんする人ひとはお酒さけを飲のんではいけません。 People who drive must not drink alcohol. (formal)
  • 食たべものを残のこしちゃだめですよ。 You shouldn't leave food on your plate.
V₁-て から、V₂
after doing V₁, do V₂
V₁-て + から + V₂ (emphasises completion of V₁ before V₂ begins)
States a strict sequence of actions — V₁ must be completed before V₂ starts. Stronger than the て-form chain alone; the から makes the order explicit and necessary.
  • 「いただきます」と言いってから、食事しょくじをはじめます。 After saying "itadakimasu," we start eating.
  • 飲のみ物ものはかんぱいしてから、飲のみましょう。 Let's drink after we've made a toast.
  • 注文ちゅうもんしてから、料理りょうりを決きめましょう。 Let's decide on the food after we've ordered drinks.
V₁-て / V₁-ないで + V₂
do V₂ with/by doing V₁ · do V₂ without doing V₁
V₁-て + V₂ (with, by means of) · V₁-ないで + V₂ (without doing V₁)
Describes the manner or accompanying condition of V₂. V-て describes a state or action done simultaneously; V-ないで means V₂ is done in the absence of V₁. Both answer "how?" — how do you eat this?
  • たれをつけて食たべてください。 Please eat it with the dipping sauce.
  • コーヒーにミルクを入いれないで飲のみます。 I drink coffee without putting milk in.
  • しょうゆをかけないで、そのまま食たべてみてください。 Try eating it as is, without adding soy sauce.
V-る と、___
when/if you do V, [result] happens
V (dictionary form) + と、[natural / inevitable result]
Expresses a natural, automatic, or habitual result — when X happens, Y inevitably follows. The result cannot be a request, command, or volitional action (use たら / ば for those cases). Very common for food instructions.
  • とうふステーキにマヨネーズをつけて食たべると、おいしいです。 If you eat the tofu steak with mayonnaise, it's delicious.
  • しょうゆをかけすぎると、しょっぱいですよ。 If you put too much soy sauce, it'll be too salty.
  • 春はるになると、桜さくらがさきます。 When spring comes, the cherry blossoms bloom.
Note: the と-conditional cannot be followed by requests (〜てください), commands, invitations, or expressions of will. Use たら or ば for those.
Conversation

Episode 4 Highlight

Key lines
Episode 4 いただきます
Rupa
これ、どうやって食たべますか。 How do you eat this?
Abe
まず、「いただきます」と言いってから食たべます。それから、たれをつけて食たべてください。 First, say "itadakimasu" before eating. Then please eat it with the dipping sauce.
Rupa
マヨネーズは？入いれなくてもいいですか。 What about mayonnaise? Is it OK without it?
Abe
マヨネーズをつけて食たべると、とてもおいしいですよ。でも、しょうゆをかけすぎちゃだめです。 If you eat it with mayonnaise, it's really delicious. But don't put on too much soy sauce.
